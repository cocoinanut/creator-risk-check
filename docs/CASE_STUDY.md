# Product case study

## Problem and scope

Before a creator partnership, a reviewer needs to distinguish identity, reported concerns, available evidence and missing coverage. This project explores that workflow as a portfolio prototype. It does not claim validated risk prediction, measured time savings or employer adoption.

## Decisions embodied in the prototype

1. Confirm the intended identity before research. A selected profile identifies the user's search target; it does not authenticate ownership.
2. Keep fictional review examples separate from public-source research. The demo explains the intended experience without manufacturing facts about real people.
3. Show evidence, dates and coverage gaps beside conclusions. Index timestamps are distinguished from publication dates.
4. Preserve relevant historical context. Serious historical concerns remain visible; low-risk older items can collapse without disappearing.
5. Separate system suggestions from human decisions. Reviewers can record an override and reason without rewriting underlying evidence or scores.
6. Withhold real-person scores. Public search coverage is incomplete, so Live returns a null score rather than interpreting silence as safety.

## What can be demonstrated

Three sample cases, transparent six-dimension weights, a severe-risk example rule, evidence drawers, browser persistence, bilingual navigation, input disambiguation and a separate public-source workspace.

## What remains to validate

The project owner confirmed successful real-source retrieval acceptance on 2026-10-09; credentials remain private and are excluded from GitHub. Remaining validation includes broader Live interaction testing with real results, independent usability sessions, reviewer agreement and any claimed operational benefit.
